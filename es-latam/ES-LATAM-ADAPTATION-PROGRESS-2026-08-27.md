### Important methodological finding

The Spanish terminology corpus is not allowed to rewrite the English Handbook.

When the English Handbook makes a substantive clinical, political, legal, or institutional claim not supported by the Spanish terminology corpus, the adaptation preserves it as an upstream source claim and flags the gap.

### Upstream questions surfaced

The adaptation work revealed questions that belong to the English policy/content layer rather than to translation:

- appeals vs. `no appeals`
- progressive discipline vs. immediate/final removal authority
- operational meaning of `Reduced expectations when needed`
- accommodation documentation requirements
- operational scope of `No cops`
- threshold in the `new rule` expulsion clause
- meaning/enforcement scope of `gender griefing`

These are being preserved as upstream questions rather than silently fixed in Spanish.

### Source-set note

The Chilean sources were selected because they combined recent publication, institutional/community provenance, neuroaffirmative language, disability-rights framing, educational relevance, practical accessibility guidance, and strong online availability.

The UNLP source adds an Argentine higher-education and disability-rights perspective.

The source mix is an evidence-selection result, not a claim of national superiority.

### AI-assisted workflow

The work is openly AI-assisted.

OpenAI GPT is being used as the primary editorial and QA model. This reflects the project owner's experience with regional Latin American Spanish in this workflow and is documented as a project-specific editorial judgment, not a general benchmark claim.

LLM output is not treated as source authority.
